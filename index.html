Selected Case Studies

Physician UX for Alzheimer’s Biomarker Ordering & Communication

Phase: Development & Workflow Design

Research Goal: Understand physician decision-making, workflows, and communication strategies when ordering and explaining Alzheimer’s disease biomarker tests.

My Role: Led mixed-methods UX research, including survey design and qualitative interviews, and synthesized findings into product and workflow recommendations.

Methods: Surveys, in-depth qualitative interviews, workflow analysis

Approach: Used surveys to identify broad patterns in physician practices, then used interviews to understand the reasoning behind those patterns, including barriers in ordering, interpretation, and patient communication.

Key Insights: Physicians varied in how they interpreted and communicated biomarker results and needed clearer workflow support for test selection and explanation.

Impact: Findings informed improvements to tools and workflows supporting biomarker ordering and communication.

UX Lead – Avian Flu Detection Tool for Farmers

Phase: Implementation & Adoption (Ongoing)

Research Goal: Evaluate usability, adoption barriers, and facilitators for integrating a biosensor tool into farmers’ real-world workflows.

My Role: Leading UX research to assess usability, perceived usefulness, and feasibility within day-to-day agricultural routines.

Methods: Qualitative interviews (in progress), usability evaluation (planned)

Approach: Conducting interviews to understand whether the biosensor is user-friendly, useful, and practical to keep within existing workflows, while identifying barriers and facilitators to adoption.

Key Focus Areas: Workflow integration, usability, perceived usefulness, environmental constraints, and implementation barriers.

Impact: Research will inform improvements to support adoption, usability, and alignment with real-world farming practices.

Alzheimer’s Risk Report Usability Testing

Phase: Development, Evaluation & Implementation

Research Goal: Evaluate understanding, psychological impact, and cultural appropriateness of biomarker risk reports used to return Alzheimer’s disease biomarker results.

My Role: Led mixed-methods research combining surveys, usability testing, and qualitative interviews.

Methods: Surveys, usability testing, qualitative interviews

Approach: Used surveys to assess patterns in understanding and emotional response, and combined interviews and usability testing to identify comprehension challenges, trust issues, and barriers to effective communication.

Key Insights: Users varied in how they understood risk information and responded emotionally to results, making clarity, psychological sensitivity, and cultural appropriateness critical to the report design.

Impact: Findings informed refinements to the report and are supporting implementation of biomarker result return to participants.
