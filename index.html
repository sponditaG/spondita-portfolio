Selected Case Studies

Physician UX for Alzheimer’s Biomarker Ordering & Communication

Phase: Development & Workflow Understanding (Ongoing)

Research Goal: Understand patient and physician experiences with biomarker testing, including satisfaction, barriers, and facilitators to ordering tests for Alzheimer’s disease diagnosis.

My Role: Leading mixed-methods UX research, including survey design and qualitative interviews.

Methods: Surveys, in-depth qualitative interviews (ongoing)

Approach: Using surveys to assess overall satisfaction and patterns in biomarker testing experiences, followed by interviews to explore barriers and facilitators in ordering and explaining results.

Key Focus Areas: Ordering workflows, communication of results, and institutional constraints such as time and clinical processes.

Expected Insights: Identify whether challenges are driven by ordering complexity, difficulty explaining results to patients, or broader institutional barriers.

UX Lead – Avian Flu Detection Tool for Farmers

Phase: Implementation & Adoption (Ongoing)

Research Goal: Evaluate barriers and facilitators to adoption of a biosensor tool for detecting avian flu in farm settings.

My Role: Leading UX research to assess usability, workflow fit, and real-world feasibility.

Methods: Qualitative interviews (in progress), usability evaluation (planned)

Approach: Conducting interviews to understand whether farmers perceive the tool as useful, easy to use, and feasible to integrate into their daily workflows.

Key Focus Areas: Adoption likelihood, usability of the biosensor, workflow integration, and environmental constraints.

Expected Insights: Identify what modifications are needed to make the tool user-friendly and ensure it aligns with real-world farming practices.

Alzheimer’s Risk Report Usability Testing

Phase: Development, Evaluation & Implementation

Research Goal: Evaluate how users understand biomarker risk results, including psychological impact and cultural appropriateness of risk communication.

My Role: Led mixed-methods UX research combining surveys, usability testing, and qualitative interviews.

Methods: Surveys, usability testing, qualitative interviews

Approach: Assessed comprehension, emotional response, and user experience across different risk presentation formats.

Key Insights: Users interpret risk information differently depending on numerical literacy; individuals with lower numerical literacy showed a preference for bar-based visualizations over numeric formats.

Psychological Findings: No evidence of psychological harm; in some cases, risk information influenced behavior and engagement.

Impact: Findings informed refinements to risk report design to improve clarity, accessibility, and user understanding.
